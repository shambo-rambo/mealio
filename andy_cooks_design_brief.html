Design brief
Andy Cooks — Brand & UI System
Based on andy-cooks.com — a Shopify-hosted creator brand site. Use this brief to mirror the aesthetic in your recipe import PWA.

Surface rules
Follow this strict surface hierarchy across your PWA:
Nav / app bar#1a1a1a — dark ink. White text.
Page background#ffffff or #f1efe8 off-white. Alternates per section.
Cards#ffffff surface, 0.5px stone border, subtle box-shadow (1px 2px 6px rgba(0,0,0,.05)).
Input fields#f1efe8 fill, 1px #d3d1c7 border. No radius on mobile — uses 8px on desktop.
Badges / pills#1a1a1a background, white text. "New" badge on recipe cards.
Footer#1a1a1a full-bleed. White/muted text.
Type scale
The site uses a classic serif/sans pairing. Display headings use a high-contrast serif — likely Canela or Freight Display. Body copy is clean sans-serif — likely Neue Haas Grotesk or system-safe Inter. Replicate with: heading → Georgia/Times fallback, body → Inter or system-ui.

Font stack for your PWA
Use these CSS font families directly:
--font-display: 'Playfair Display', Georgia, serif;
--font-body: 'Inter', system-ui, sans-serif;
--font-mono: 'JetBrains Mono', monospace;
Playfair Display is freely available on Google Fonts and is the closest free match to Andy's display serif. Inter handles all UI chrome. Both load well on mobile.

Full-bleed hero: 0px
No pill-shaped components anywhere. The design deliberately avoids over-rounded UI — it feels editorial, not app-y. Keep radii small or absent. Images inside cards use overflow:hidden on the parent to clip naturally.
Motion
Minimal animation. Hover transitions at 150ms ease. Card image zoom on hover at scale(1.02) over 300ms ease-out. No bouncy or spring animations. Page transitions: simple opacity fade at 200ms. No skeleton loaders — just a simple background colour pulse. Stick to CSS transitions on transform and opacity only.
Brand voice
Andy Cooks positions as a professional chef who's also genuinely approachable. The language is direct and warm without being cutesy. Technical but never condescending.
Tagline registerCasual, first-person, relational. "Hey babe, what's for dinner?" speaks directly to a partner in a shared kitchen.
Recipe introsLead with what makes the recipe special or tricky. Direct instruction before emotional framing.
Ingredient notesBold ingredient name. One paragraph explanation — why it matters, what to substitute. No bullet lists.
Chef tipsNamed subsections ("Start guanciale in a cold pan"). Technique-first, reason second. Short paragraphs.
FAQsBold question as header. Direct, confident answer. Often ends with a practical takeaway.
CTAsArrow-forward ("See recipe →", "View all recipes →"). No "click here" or "learn more".
Social proofUnderstated — star rating shown but not pushed.
PWA copy guidelines
Mirror these patterns in your own UI copy to feel consistent with the source brand:
Empty states"No recipes yet — add one to get started." Short, no emojis.
Loading states"Fetching recipe..." not "Loading..." — be specific.
Error messages"Couldn't import from that URL." Cause + resolution in plain language.
Success states"Recipe saved." Just say it — no celebration copy.
ButtonsVerbs: "Import", "Save", "Add to plan" — not "Submit" or "OK".
Section labelsALL-CAPS tracked small-caps for meta labels (Prep · Cook · Serves). Sentence case for everything else.
Photography & imagery
All food photography is warm-toned, natural-light, close-up. Backgrounds are soft, textured (stone bench, linen, timber). The colour palette of the photography IS the accent palette of the site — let images carry warmth; keep UI chrome neutral. For imported recipes, use the thumbnail from the source platform and apply a consistent aspect ratio crop (3:4 portrait for cards, 16:9 for detail hero).
